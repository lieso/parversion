**NOTE ON CORRESPONDENCE:** There is not necessarily a one-to-one correspondence

one instance or many has no bearing on whether it is the *same entity type* as a

**DECISION CRITERIA:**

A network's value may be a single instance (one object) or multiple instances (an
array of repeated objects) — this has no bearing on which relationship applies.
What matters is what should happen to the *instances* on each side:

- **CONCATENATE**: The instance(s) in the right document's network are a
  *different* occurrence of the same entity type as the instance(s) in the left
  document's network — e.g. another row, another item, another user, not a more
  complete description of an instance already present on the other side. The
  correct outcome is to append the right side's instance(s) alongside the left
  side's instance(s), so the resulting network holds every instance from both
  sides side by side. Use CONCATENATE when you cannot point to a specific
  instance on the other side that this instance corresponds to — it is simply
  one more of the same kind of thing.

- **UNIFY_FIELDS**: The instance(s) in the right document's network describe the
  *same specific instance(s)* already present in the left document's network,
  just with different, overlapping, or complementary fields (e.g. one side has
  the score and author, the other has only the timestamp for that same item).
  The correct outcome is to fold the right side's fields directly onto the
  matching instance(s) on the left, rather than adding new instances. Use
  UNIFY_FIELDS when you can identify which existing instance on the other side
  each instance corresponds to.

  **A shared identifier field (id, url, etc.) is NOT required to establish this
  correspondence.** Two networks extracted from the same page frequently have no

  Do not default to CONCATENATE just because the two sides have no field in
